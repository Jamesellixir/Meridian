## 1. Problem and product

People need help turning a goal into completed work, and plans must adapt when confirmed constraints change. Meridian is a personal agent with its own calendar, tasks, notes, and inspectable confirmed memory.

## 2. One concrete flow

Use the two-hour AI meetup example: enter a goal and busy periods, prepare an agenda and tasks, review each proposed note/task/event write, approve its revision, and inspect persisted outputs and individual receipts. The working demo must demonstrate each claimed action.

## 3. Distinctive behavior

The intended innovation is dependency-aware replanning. Correct availability to mornings only; identify the affected schedule; propose a checked before/after revision while keeping unrelated agenda/tasks. Already approved events require a new review before movement.

## 4. How Jac supports the system

Explain the actual final Jac frontend, coordinator/tool flow, shared graph/state persistence, and dependency relationships. Name the installed Jac/Jaseci capabilities only after implementation is confirmed. Include a verified language measurement and its counting method; do not equate file counts with meaningful Jac use.

## 5. Demonstration and trust

Show actual tool activity, exact approval, a receipt, and reload persistence. Explain the verified behavior for rejection, stale revisions, conflicts/no-slot, and retries. Do not claim general browser automation, external calendar integration, web research, or unattended background execution.

## 6. Impact, limits, and next steps

Describe the practical value of retained work and reviewable changes. State what the prototype currently does and its measured limitations. Keep future integrations clearly labelled as future work. Do not claim guaranteed novelty, ranking, or performance without evidence.
